Delayed triangulation vs inherited depth

During a pure-rotation segment no parallax exists, so a feature born there cannot be triangulated. Delayed waits for parallax and triangulates honestly (deferring — no estimate — if rotation persists to the track's end). Inherited immediately assigns the median depth of neighbouring map points: an unconstrained guess. Both strategies share the same camera trajectory here (inherited points are excluded from PnP and bundle adjustment), so the comparison isolates the depth strategy's effect on ground contact.

Reading the result

Delayed is honest about ignorance: it reports "no estimate" for most rotation-born ground tracks rather than fabricating a position. Inherited always has an answer, but it is borrowed from neighbours, not measured — on a flat ground plane the guess can look plausible, yet it carries no parallax evidence and would mislead any virtual object placed from it. If inherited points were allowed into the optimization, their unconstrained depth would also corrupt the shared cameras.
